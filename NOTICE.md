# Third-party material

The MIT license in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) covers SMAIRT's own source, documentation, and scaffold
assets. It does **not** relicense the third-party data redistributed under `demos/`, which keeps its
own terms. This file inventories that material.

Nothing under `src/` vendors third-party code. `legacy/cookiecutter/` is SMAIRT's own retired
template, under the same MIT license.

## Redistributed data

Every payload below is small, cited, and accompanied by a `README.md` in its own directory
recording at least the source, the transformation applied, and a SHA-256. Most also record the query
or accessions and a retrieval date; the Puromycin dataset has no retrieval date, because it was
copied from an R installation rather than fetched from a service. Those per-directory records are
authoritative; this table is the index.

Synthetic payloads under `data/synthetic/` are generated by the demos themselves and carry no
third-party terms. Their records list the seed and generating script rather than a source.

## Two disclosed limitations

Recorded here because they bound what the affected demos can claim, and a reader should not have to
find them in a script:

- `yeast_ppi.csv` mixes STRING interaction scores with **locally asserted** essentiality labels — a
  hardcoded gene set, not an export from the *Saccharomyces* Genome Deletion Project. The demo's
  Precision@3 metric is scored against those labels.
- `uniprot_benchmark.csv` sequences are **truncated to 200 residues**, so a transmembrane helix
  occurring later is absent. Reported accuracy is for the truncated sequences.
